# Research Failure Defenses

Use these when evaluating a weak or surprising conclusion; they are not additional phases.

- Familiarity mistaken for evidence: inspect current primary sources for material claims.
- Scope drift: compare the conclusion to the accepted question and state what remains unanswered.
- Context loss: keep provenance, budgets, changed conclusions, and next read targets in topic state.
  The root can read original sources; compact handoffs instead of prohibiting source inspection.
- Unsupported enthusiasm: identify the weakest assumption and whether the experiment actually tests
  it. Passing every experiment is neither proof of rigor nor a reason to force a failure.
- Domain gaps: seek relevant practitioner evidence or ask a consequential missing question, without
  mandatory phase approvals.
- False corroboration: trace evidence to independent origins and methods. Repeated summaries are
  not independent confirmation; newer contrary evidence can overturn earlier support.

Before concluding, check whether any unresolved gap could reverse the recommendation. If so, expose
that dependency or narrow the answer. Repeated attempts never reduce the evidence standard.
